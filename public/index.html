      Starting out as a maths ECT

      Your first years of teaching are your induction. Where you do them matters more than almost anything else on an advert, so this site leans towards schools that can support you and shows you their results before you apply.

      In England: what every ECT is entitled to

      • Two years of induction if you work full time, assessed against the Teachers’ Standards. Part-time ECTs take proportionally longer.
      • A reduced timetable: 10% less teaching in year one and 5% less in year two, on top of your normal PPA time.
      • An induction mentor and an induction tutor, and a funded training programme based on the Initial Teacher Training and Early Career Framework.
      • Fixed-term and maternity-cover posts can count towards induction, as long as the post lasts at least a term.

      State schools, academies, free schools and sixth form colleges can all offer statutory induction. An independent school can only do so through an appropriate body, so ask before you apply.

      Teaching in Wales

      • New teachers are still called NQTs in Wales, and adverts often say “NQTs welcome”. The site treats NQT and ECT as the same thing.
      • Induction lasts about three school terms (380 sessions), and you must register with the Education Workforce Council before it can start.
      • Pay follows the Welsh pay document, not England’s. For 2026/27 Wales is moving to a single pay scale, so check the figure in each advert.
      • Welsh-medium schools teach in Welsh, so maths posts there usually need you to speak Welsh. Dual stream schools teach in both languages. The map and school profiles show which is which.
      • Wales does not publish exam results school by school, so Welsh schools have no GCSE or A level figures here. My Local School and Estyn have each school’s official information.

      Reading an advert

      MPS, MPR, M1–M6
      Main pay scale. Adverts open to ECTs usually say this. “UPS only” or a TLR means an experienced teacher.
      TLR
      Teaching and learning responsibility payment, for roles like second in department or KS5 lead. Rarely an ECT post.
      KS5, post-16
      A level teaching. Some schools give ECTs a Year 12 class in their first year; ask how much.
      Mentoring, ECF, ITTECF
      Signs the school has thought about induction. The site picks these words out of each advert.

      Questions worth asking at interview

      • Who would my mentor be, and how much time do they get for it?
      • Which key stages would I teach in my first year? Is there any A level or further maths?
      • How are classes set, and which scheme of work does the department follow?
      • How many maths teachers are there, and how many joined in the last two years?
      • How does the behaviour policy work in practice in a maths lesson?

      How to read the school results

      GCSE maths grade 4+
      Share of pupils at the end of Year 11 who got at least a 4 (a standard pass) in GCSE maths. Grade 5+ is a strong pass. From the DfE key stage 4 data, 2025/26, compared with the school’s council area, its region and England.
      A level average
      The DfE average point score per A level entry, shown as a grade (for example B−). From the DfE 16 to 19 data, 2024/25.
      UCAS points
      The DfE scale (A* = 60 … E = 10) converts exactly to UCAS tariff points (A* = 56 … E = 16): tariff = 0.8 × points + 8. “For best three” uses the DfE’s average of each student’s best three A levels, so a B− average is about 112.
      A level maths uptake
      Share of the school’s A level students who took maths, and further maths, with the share of maths entries graded A* or A. A high uptake means a strong post-16 maths department to teach in.
      Progress 8
      Not published for 2025 or 2026 because pupils have no key stage 2 results from 2020 and 2021.

          About the data

          • Schools: open state-funded secondary, all-through and 16–19 schools, sixth form colleges and FE colleges in England, from Get Information About Schools, and maintained secondary and middle schools in Wales, from the Welsh Government’s school list.
          • Results: DfE school-level data on Explore Education Statistics (GCSE 2025/26). England only.
          • Job details, closing dates and ECT flags come from each job board. Always check the original advert before applying.
